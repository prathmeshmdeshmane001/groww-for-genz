# Gen Z investing survey — paste into Google Forms

**Tap-only. 12 questions, ~90 seconds, zero required typing.**

Every question is multiple choice or checkboxes. The one free-text box is at the very end and
clearly optional, placed after the point where people have already mentally finished the form.
People who care will fill it. People who don't will submit anyway — which is the whole point.

Depth comes from the 3 interviews, not from this. Survey gives you **counts**. Interviews give
you **quotes**. Don't make the survey do the interview's job.

**Form title:** 90-second money question (for a case study I'm doing)

**Form description:**
> I'm doing a product case study on why people our age open an investing account and then
> never actually invest. All tap, no typing, 90 seconds. Anonymous.
> "I haven't started" is the most useful answer you can give me.

**Settings:** collect email = OFF. Make Q1–Q9 required (they're all taps, so it costs nothing).
Leave Q10–Q12 optional.

---

### 1. How old are you?
*Multiple choice*
- 18–20
- 21–23
- 24–26
- 27+

### 2. Right now, your money situation is closest to:
*Multiple choice*
- Student, no income of my own
- Student with internship / part-time / freelance income
- First full-time job (under 2 years in)
- Working 2+ years
- Between things right now

### 3. Which one is true for you?
*Multiple choice — your segmentation question, everything else gets cut by it*
- I've never opened any investing/demat account
- I opened an account but have never actually invested anything
- I invested once or twice, then stopped
- I invest regularly (SIP or otherwise)
- I trade actively (intraday / F&O / options)

### 4. You just got ₹5,000 you don't need for a year. Pick what you'd *actually* do with it today.
*Multiple choice — pick one only*
- Just leave it in my bank account
- FD or RD
- Start or add to a mutual fund SIP
- Buy an index fund or ETF
- Buy a stock I've heard about
- Buy digital gold
- Buy crypto
- Spend it on something I've been wanting
- Give it to my parents / family
- Genuinely no idea

**This is the most important question in the form.** You are measuring *spread*, not
correctness. If your 15 friends scatter across 8 of these options, that's your choice-paralysis
finding in your own data. If "leave it in my bank account" and "genuinely no idea" together top
the chart, that's your dormancy finding. Either outcome gives you a number for the 1-pager.

### 5. What's actually stopping you from investing more than you do now?
*Multiple choice — force one answer, no "all of the above"*
- I don't know what to buy
- I'm scared of losing the money
- I don't think I have enough for it to be worth it
- I don't trust these apps or the market
- I keep meaning to and just never get around to it
- Too confusing, I gave up trying to figure it out
- Nothing's stopping me, I'm already doing it

### 6. If you invest ₹500 in a stock and sell it next week, roughly how much do you think you'd pay in charges?
*Multiple choice*
- Nothing, it's free
- Around ₹5
- Around ₹20
- Around ₹60
- More than ₹60
- No idea

**Sleeper question.** The real answer is **about ₹18.22 on a ₹500 round trip** (3.6%) once the flat
₹15.93 depository fee, STT, GST and stamp duty land — so the "Around ₹20" option is the correct one.
*(Corrected 17 Sep 2026. An earlier draft said ~₹60; that figure came from a Reddit user who had made
five or six trades in a day, not one round trip.)* If most of your sample picks "nothing" or "no idea", you have just
proven the cost-transparency problem yourself, with a clean quotable stat — instead of borrowing
one Reddit thread for it.

*Checkboxes — this is your feature-idea generator, whatever tops it is a screen in your app*

### 11. Anything else you want to say about money or investing? *(optional, skip freely)*
*Paragraph, NOT required*

Placed last on purpose. By here they've already answered everything that matters, so a blank
box can't cost you the response. The 2–3 people who do type something will give you your best
line — and it's free.

### 12. Can I ask you 3 quick questions on a call? (10 min) *(optional)*

## How to send it

Do this in the next 30 minutes. Everything else you build today gets better the longer
responses have been coming in.

**Don't** drop "pls fill 🙏" in a group. That gets 4 responses.

**Do** send one-to-one, and lead with the low cost:

> yaar quick one — doing a product case study on why people our age open a demat account and
> then never actually invest. 90 seconds, all tap, no typing. and honestly the "i haven't
> started" answers are the ones i need most 🙏 [link]

"90 seconds, all tap, no typing" is doing real work in that message. Say it.

Send to 25–30 people to land 15–18 responses. Deliberately hunt the people you think have
**not** started — they're the ones everyone else's research misses, and they're your whole thesis.

---

## Interview guide — 3 people, 15 minutes each

**This is where the descriptive data comes from.** The survey deliberately doesn't ask for it.

Run these once ~8 responses are in, so you can pick the right people off Q3 and Q12.

**Who to pick:**
1. Someone who **opened an account and did nothing** ← the single most valuable interview
   available to you. This person is your thesis, and nobody else applying will have talked to one.
2. Someone who **invested once or twice and stopped** — shows you where the drop-off sits.
3. Someone who **trades / does F&O** — shows you what the pull feels like from inside.

**How to run it — don't over-script.** Don't ask what they think. Ask them to show you.

1. "Open the app right now. Walk me through what you see and what you'd do next."
   → Then shut up. Let the silence sit. The pauses are the finding.
2. "Take me back to the day you opened the account. What made you do it *that* day?"
   → Then: "And what happened after?" The gap between those two answers is your funnel leak.
3. "I'm giving you ₹500. You have 2 minutes to invest it. Go."
   → Watch whether they freeze, and **time it**. "Took 4 minutes and gave up" is a killer line.
4. Ask Q6's real answer out loud: "how much do you think that just cost you in charges?"
   → Their face is the finding.

**Record it if they're okay with it.** You won't remember the good quotes, and one exact
verbatim beats a paragraph of your own analysis in a 1-pager.

---

## What to do with the results

- **Q4's spread** → your own evidence for choice paralysis. Quote the number of different
  answers you got.
- **Q6** → your own evidence for the cost-transparency problem.
- **Q3 × Q7 cross-tab** → your drop-off picture from your own sample.
- **Q10's top answers** → the screens you build.
- **Q5, Q10 and the interview quotes** → your **eval set**. You test the app against whether it
  actually fixes what your 15 friends said blocked them. That's a far stronger eval story than
  generic heuristics, and evals are the deliverable most candidates will fumble.
