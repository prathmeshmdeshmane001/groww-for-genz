Field script · Gen Z investing · 3 sessions × 25 min

Interview script

Print one copy per person. Structure to follow, not words to read out. The goal is what they do, not what they say about it.

Two measurements matter more than everything else: the charges guess before and after, and seconds to first investment. If a session runs short, protect those and drop the rest.

Before they arrive

  • App link tested in incognito, open in a tab, sitting on question 1
  • Recording ready — phone voice memo is fine, or screen record if remote
  • Their survey answers open in another tab, so you can compare live
  • This sheet printed, pen in hand
  • Phone on silent. Yours, not theirs
The one rule that decides whether this is worth doing: you are testing the app, not defending it. Every time you explain something, you delete a finding. If they can't work it out, that is the result.

Who to pick

Priority 1Opened, never investedSix of your seventeen. This person is your entire thesis and nobody else interviewing for this role will have talked to one.
Priority 2Invested once, then stoppedThree of seventeen. They've been through the drop-off you're trying to fix, so they can tell you where it happened.
Priority 3Trades or invests regularlyTells you what the pull feels like from inside, and whether your app is patronising to someone who's past this stage.

Six rules for you, not them

01
Count to five after every question.The silence feels unbearable to you and normal to them. The best answer almost always arrives after the pause you wanted to fill.
02
Never explain the app."What does this mean?" → "What do you think it means?" Then wait. Their guess is the data.
03
Never defend it.Criticism → "Say more about that." Nothing else. Not "ah that's because…"
04
Ask about the past, never the future."What did you do last time?" predicts behaviour. "Would you use this?" predicts nothing — everyone says yes to a friend.
05
Watch their hands, not their face.Where the thumb hovers before it taps is the hesitation. Note it. They won't mention it and won't remember it.
06
Write the exact words.Paraphrase is lost evidence. One verbatim quote is worth a paragraph of your own analysis in the write-up.

Part 0 — Setting up

2 min

Get consent on the record, then lower the stakes. They will want to be nice to you; your job is to make that feel unhelpful.

Ask if you can record. Say it's only for you, so you're not scribbling while they talk.
Tell them: you didn't design this to be admired, compliments are useless to you, and the most helpful thing they can do is get stuck and say so out loud. Do not say "I built this" more than once. The less ownership you show, the harsher they'll be.

Part 1 — Baseline, before they see anything

Do not open the app in this section. These answers are your before-measurement. Once they've seen the app you can never collect them again.
1 min

1. Where they're at

Confirm what the survey said, in their words. Money coming in, from what, and whether any of it currently goes anywhere.

2 min

2. The ₹5,000 question — time this

"I've just given you ₹5,000. You don't need it for a year. What do you actually do with it?" Start a timer. Say nothing until they answer. If they ask a clarifying question, say "whatever you'd really do."

You want three things: how long they took, their exact words, and whether they changed their mind mid-answer. Hesitation here is the choice-paralysis finding in the flesh.

Seconds to answer · verbatim · did they waver
1 min

3. The charges question — your key measurement

"You put ₹500 into a stock. You sell it next week. Roughly how much do you think you pay in charges?" Make them commit to a number. "No idea" is a valid answer — write it down as that.

Real answer is about ₹18, mostly a flat ₹15.93 DP charge. You'll ask this again at the end. The gap between the two answers is your headline eval result.

Their guess · before
3 min

4. The last time they tried

"Take me back to the last time you thought about investing, or actually opened an app to do it. What happened?"

Then keep pulling on the thread. "And then what?" · "What stopped you?" · "What were you doing right before that?"

If they opened an account: what made them do it that day, and what happened after. The gap between those two answers is your funnel leak, described by someone who fell through it.

Where exactly it stalled
1 min

5. The debt question

"Do you owe anyone money right now? EMI, BNPL, education loan, credit card, a friend?"

This is testing someone else's theory — that debt is a sharper pain than investing for this age group, and a better place to start. If all three say yes and it's clearly what they worry about, you need a line about it in the write-up. If they say no, you've closed it with your own evidence.

Owes / doesn't · does it worry them

Part 2 — The app

Hand over the phone, or ask them to share their screen. Say nothing beyond the task. No tour, no "so basically it…". Start your timer on the first tap.
6 min

Task 1 — "Invest ₹500. Go."

That's the whole instruction. Then be quiet and watch.

Ask them to narrate: "Say what you're thinking, even if it's boring." If they go silent for a while, prompt once with "what are you looking at?" — not "what's confusing?", which puts the answer in their mouth.

When it goes wrong

  • They get stuck → let them stay stuck a full 30 seconds. Count it. That number is a finding.
  • They ask you what to tap → "what would you do if I wasn't here?"
  • They blast through in 15 seconds → "what did you just buy?" If they can't say, they didn't read it, and that is a result.
  • They say "this is nice" → "what's the most annoying thing about it?"
Seconds to invested · help requests · every hesitation and where
3 min

Task 2 — "How much would it cost you to sell next week?"

Don't point at anything. You're testing whether the cost screen is findable, not whether it's readable once found.

Once they find it, ask what surprised them. If they flip to the other tabs unprompted, note it — that's the comparison doing its job on its own.

Found it? · how long · what they said when they saw the number
3 min

Task 3 — the red day

On the finished screen, point only at "Skip ahead three weeks". Say nothing else.

"You're down. What do you do?" Then shut up completely. Whatever they tap first is the answer — hold or sell.

After they choose, ask "why?" Then check whether they noticed "What if it doesn't come back?" at all, and whether they'd trust the app more or less for including it.

Held or sold · their reason · reaction to the honest bit

Part 3 — After

2 min

6. Ask the charges question again

"Same question as at the start. ₹500 in a stock, sold next week. How much in charges?"

This is your evals deliverable. Wrong → right means the screen worked. Wrong → still wrong means it didn't, which is just as useful and far more honest than a pass mark you awarded yourself.

Guess before → guess after
3 min

7. The closing four

"If you had to describe this app to a friend in one sentence, what would you say?" The best question in the whole script. If their sentence isn't roughly your pitch, your positioning is wrong, not theirs.
"What confused you? Be specific — which screen, which word."
"If you could change one thing, what?"
"Honestly — would you have finished that on your own, at home, with nobody watching?" The only future-tense question worth asking, because it invites a no.
Their one-sentence description, exact words
2 min

8. Two strategy questions — last, after everything else is banked

"If this app cost ₹99 a month, would you pay for it?" Watch the face before the words. India's one real attempt at paid retail guidance charges ₹249/month — which is 50% of what your user invests in a year at ₹500 a month. This question turns that from someone else's evidence into yours.
"Would you want it to show you other options too?" The highest-stakes design bet in the app. A competitor built to kill decision fatigue ended up giving users 10–15 funds; that drift starts with exactly this request. If they ask for more, the one-default bet has a crack in it and the write-up should say so.
Pay yes/no + reaction · wants more options yes/no
Watch for, don't ask: does anyone try to buy a specific stock? A funded competitor is betting its whole product on "stocks first" for this exact user. If yours reach for that unprompted, note it — but never prompt it, or you've planted the idea and lost the finding.

The real risk in today's sessions

  • You have just read three competitors' decks. You will walk in wanting to confirm things.
  • The entire value of these sessions is that they can prove you wrong — about the cost screen, about one default, about the horizon question being the right opener.
  • Your friends will help you be right. The stopwatch won't. Trust the stopwatch.

Five minutes while it's fresh

Before you speak to anyone else, write down:

  • The one moment they hesitated longest. Screen, and what they were looking at.
  • The best thing they said, word for word, no cleaning up.
  • One thing you'd change before the next session — and if it's a five-minute fix, make it. Two people seeing v1 and one seeing v2 is a stronger evals story than three people seeing the same flaw.
Don't fix everything between sessions. Change one thing at a time, or you won't know which change did what — and "we moved X after P1 couldn't find it, and P2 found it in four seconds" is the single most credible sentence you can put in the evals deliverable.
