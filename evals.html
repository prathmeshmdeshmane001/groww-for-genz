Two Questions, Two Evaluation Layers

Layer Core Question Evaluation Method
Layer 1 (Automated) Does it calculate arithmetic correctly and handle every state permutation? Automated Node test suite (23 assertions) & real browser crawl.
Layer 2 (Cognitive) Does it change user perception of fees and drive intentional holding behavior? Recorded think-aloud usability interviews against a measured baseline.

Layer 1 — Automated Financial Arithmetic

The foundation of the product is pre-trade cost transparency. If fee numbers are wrong, the value proposition collapses. The suite asserts logic directly from the shipped code:

  • The Anchor: ₹500 equity round-trip costs exactly ₹18.22; ₹100 costs ₹16.39 (16.4% due to flat CDSL depository fees).
  • Compounding Fee Drag: Mutual fund expense ratios compound daily on a growing NAV balance (+53% higher than naive principal models).
  • Exit Loads: Balanced Advantage 1% exit fee bites within 12 months and is waived after; index and liquid exit loads follow statutory rules.
  • FD Benchmark: Matches exact SBI retail term deposit slabs (1yr: 6.25%, 2yr: 6.40%, 3yr: 6.30%, 5yr: 6.05%, 6mo: 5.65%).
  • Sanity Grid: Tested across 6 amounts (₹100 to ₹1,00,000) × 6 timelines with zero NaNs or negative balances.

Layer 2 — User Testing & Behavioral Cognitive Shifts

Two recorded 35-minute think-aloud sessions were conducted against v1:

Metric Prakhar (21, In-ICP, Never Invested) Pulin (Out-of-ICP, Active Trader)
Charges Guess (Pre-Test) "Under ₹5" "1% at max" (₹5)
Charges (Post-Test) Measurement contaminated "Forty something... chaalees" (Moved cleanly)
Cost Screen Comprehension Failed: Read ₹43 fee as returns. Failed: Misinterpreted "DP charge" as Direct Plan.
First Red Day Reaction Held: Read recovery data, chose "Do Nothing". Held: "Waiting is the best thing ever."
The Core Behavioral Insight: A knowledge shift is not a behavioral shift. Pulin learned that charges were ₹40, but assumed "this is unavoidable, right?" This proved that showing costs alone is insufficient—the UI must explicitly explain why mutual funds bypass flat depository fees compared to stocks.
