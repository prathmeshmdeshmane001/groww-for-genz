Every decision, and what backs it

17 Sep 2026 · sources: survey n=24 · 2 interviews · SEBI Investor Survey 2025 · SEBI F&O study FY26 · 145 Groww Play reviews · Groww fund pages & MF Prime walkthrough, 16–17 Sep

Read this first. The brief says the 1-page write-up must not be created by AI, so there is deliberately no ready-made prose here — no sentences to lift. This is your decision list with the evidence attached to each one, so the writing is yours and the facts are checkable. You have 300–700 words. That is roughly six to eight of these, not seventeen. The ones marked core are the ones I would not cut.

The spine, in four moves

  1. Diagnosis — the account is not the problem, the first ₹500 is. Activation, not acquisition.
  2. Metric — stop counting signups. Count the second investment inside 30 days.
  3. Scope — a Groww app for someone post-KYC. Not a feature, not a startup.
  4. The three things it does — pick one thing by when you need the money, show what it costs before you buy, and be there on the first red day.

The decisions

01The problem is activation, not acquisitioncore

Sign-up is solved. What breaks is the gap between "account opened" and "money actually working".

Backing
  • SEBI Investor Survey 2025: 40% of investors are dormant — no fresh investment in a year.
  • Complexity and information gaps block 74% of non-investors; 27% say plainly "I don't know how to start".
  • Roughly 5.9% of broking-app installs ever reach a first trade; ~86% never finish activation.
  • Our survey: 17 of 24 are pre-activation or stalled (7 opened-never-invested, 6 never opened, 4 invested-then-stopped).
  • Prakhar, interview 1: opened Groww, finished KYC, waited for the demat to activate — "us time ke wajah se main distract ho gaya" — and never went back. Not fear. Not cost. A gap.
02The metric is the second investment within 30 dayscore

Account opened is a vanity milestone — the user has achieved nothing at that point.

Backing — the funnel has five leaks and the industry measures success at step 3 of 9. Nothing in the current journey is designed to produce a second investment. It is also the cleanest justification for putting F&O, lending, IPOs and tax filing out of scope.
03A Groww app, not a feature and not a startupcore

The brief says "create a Groww App suited for GenZ users". A standalone was considered and rejected.

Backing
  • ET Money Genius charges ₹249/month. That is ₹3,000 a year — a 4.76% expense ratio on a ₹5,000/month SIP, and 50% of the money at ₹500/month. Paid guidance cannot serve this ticket size.
  • So guidance for this user has to be free, and only an incumbent that earns elsewhere can afford to give it away. That is the "why Groww" argument, and it is why the same product outside Groww would not work.
04Organise by goal and time horizon, never by product typecore

The user says what the money is for and when they need it. The app picks the product. They are never asked to choose between a mutual fund, an index fund, an ETF and a stock.

Backing
  • 11 of 24 say nobody ever explained the difference between mutual fund, index fund, ETF and stock.
  • 20% of non-investors reject investing specifically because "it's for long term" — the product speaks in decades, this user thinks in months.
  • Goal-wise tracking is the top feature request in the 145 Play reviews read.
  • It is also why the red-day screen works: Prakhar was calm about being down because the horizon was already set. "Main already sure tha ki main ek dedh saal rukunga."
05Tell them how much. Don't ask.core

The app recommends ₹500 with a reason, and lets you type any number.

Backing — at n=24, "how much should I be investing" became the single most-cited thing nobody ever explained: 12 of 24, ahead of fund-type confusion (11), real charges (11) and tax (11). It was fourth at n=20 and first at n=24 — a genuine finding from the last four responses, and the reason this screen changed from asking to answering.
06Show the cost before the trade, in rupees, on their amountcore

This is the piece no competitor ships, including Groww's own guidance tier.

Backing
  • 17 of 24 got the charges question wrong. 15 of 24 badly underestimated. Nobody picked "no idea" — everyone was confident and wrong, which is worse than ignorance.
  • Real figure: a ₹500 share bought and sold costs ₹18.22, about 3.6%. ₹15.93 of that is a flat depository fee, so on ₹100 it is over 16%. Flat fees punish exactly the small amounts everyone is told to start with.
  • Groww MF Prime shows no cost anywhere — across eight screens of its guidance flow there is not one expense ratio, exit load or charge.
07Be there on the first red daycore

When it goes red, show how often that has happened before and what followed. Never block "sell".

Backing
  • SEBI names poor performance as 87% of the reason investors go dormant — ahead of everything else. They don't close the account, they just stop opening the app.
  • No broking app addresses this moment at all.
  • Both interviewees held. Prakhar read the 2008 (~3 years) and March 2020 (~9 months) recovery figures out loud and chose to do nothing unprompted. Pulin: "of course do nothing… waiting is the best thing ever."
08No F&O, no loans, no trending lists — and say so on screencore

The absence is the product decision, so the app states it rather than hiding it.

Backing
  • SEBI FY26: 43% of India's individual F&O traders are under 30, and 89% of them lost money. They carry 53% of the losses on 43% of the turnover.
  • Groww's own Play listing sells personal loans at 13–48% APR and Fast Exit for F&O; its bottom nav is Stocks · F&O · Mutual Funds · Loans — F&O second, funds third.
  • Zero of our 24 respondents trade actively. Mutual funds were the most common destination.
  • This is a live disagreement with named companies: Millions ships F&O at ₹20/order to the same GenZ audience; Trackk makes it the flagship — "F&O in the time it takes to make chai." Nobody has taken the other side.
09Ask about risk, and give the thrill a small, named homesupporting

Four risk levels, then a slider for how much goes into one riskier bet. Capped by nothing, warned about honestly.

Backing
  • Pulin raised risk appetite four separate times, unprompted, and proposed the mechanic himself: "apne risk ko partition karke, ki thoda sa risk mein lagao, baaki thoda safe rakho."
  • CFA Institute (non-India, directional): 61% of Gen Z investors gamble vs 29% of non-investors. The thrill-seeking is real and will not be lectured away.
  • Right now the only exciting thing in the app is F&O, which takes 89% of the under-30s who touch it. A capped, visible slice is somewhere for that to go that isn't options.
10Teach them to check us, not to trust uscore

Every jargon term is tappable, and every explainer ends with where to verify it independently.

Backing — this is the strongest cross-interview finding in the study. Two of two participants, unprompted, in the closing minutes, asked for the same thing:

Prakhar: "I don't know which is the source of truth… main sirf tere AI pe reliable nahi hona chahiye."

Pulin: "Jin factors pe market khelta hai, wo agar mujhe koi bataaye taaki main khud dekh paun — kyunki main apne aap se zyada kisko trust karunga? Kisi ko bhi nahi."

  • Survey free text: "Need a tutor, that helps me with this stuff from scratch."
  • Pulin read "DP charge" and guessed "Direct plan" — wrong, on the single line the whole cost argument rests on.
  • He also handed us the format: "har din notification aa raha hai ki ye aaj padh lo, ten minute read hai." He had already quit Zerodha Varsity for being "hafte mahine ka investment".
11Home shows what you own and why, not a returns dashboardsupporting

Pots with progress towards a goal, charges always visible, next contribution dated.

Backing — if poor performance drives 87% of dormancy, a home screen that leads with a red number manufactures the churn it is measuring. Prakhar never reopened Groww after submitting KYC; there was no reason to.
12Index funds for long horizons — and show ours losingsupporting

The app recommends a Nifty 50 index fund and prints the active funds beating it right underneath.

Backing, and the honest part
  • UTI Nifty 50 Index: 3Y +5.6% against ICICI Pru Large Cap +9.31%, Nippon India +9.71%, HDFC +7.94%. Our pick is losing over three years and the app says so.
  • The argument is not performance, it is certainty: the 0.25% fee is certain, the extra return is not, and you would have to pick next year's winner in advance every year.
  • Pulin rejected this reasoning — "it showed me numbers better but I didn't like the reasoning". Worth reporting rather than hiding: an informed user opting out is a real result.
13Compare against an FD, because that is the baseline people actually knowsupporting
Backing — SBI retail term deposit rates (under ₹3 crore, from 15 Dec 2025): 1yr 6.25%, 2yr 6.40%, 3yr 6.30%, 5yr 6.05%. The app shows the FD outcome next to its own on the same money over the same years, and states the trade plainly: an FD is guaranteed, this is not, and FD interest is taxed at your slab while fund gains under ₹1.25 lakh a year are not taxed at all.

Claims to avoid — each one is checkable and wrong

  • "Nobody guides the decision." Groww ships MF Prime: risk, horizon, age, income, any amount, a split recommendation across two funds, a "Why these funds?" panel and an AI assistant. Saying this to Groww would show you had not opened the product. The defensible version: guidance exists for people who already invest — nobody guides the first ₹500, shows its cost, or is there on the first red day.
  • "A ₹500 trade costs ₹64." It costs ₹18.22. The ₹64 Reddit case was five or six trades in a day. Quote ₹18.22 as the round trip and ₹64 only as his story.
  • "Groww is rated badly." Groww is 4.8 stars, 100M+ installs. The 55%-one-star figure was an artefact of sorting reviews newest-first. Use the theme counts, never the star split.
  • "Index funds beat active funds." Not over the last three years in this category, and the app shows that. Argue cost certainty instead.
  • Anything from the two seed decks. Public sources only.

Limits to state in your own words

The brief says assumptions are fine if you state them. Naming the limits is the credibility move.

  • n=24, convenience sample, friends. Directional, not representative.
  • Age skew: 21 of 24 are 21–23, against a brief asking about 20–26.
  • Two interviews, not three, and neither tested the final build — Prakhar saw the first prototype, Pulin an intermediate one.
  • Pulin is outside the target user — he already invests on two apps and checks his portfolio daily for fun. His criticism is real but it comes from outside the ICP.
  • Fund figures are as of 15 Sep 2026 and will drift. Growth illustrations use each fund's own 10-year record, which is the past, not a forecast.
