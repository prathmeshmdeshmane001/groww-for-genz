first500 · prototype scope · draft for review

Screen-by-screen scope

17 Sep 2026 · built from: survey n=24 · 2 interviews · SEBI 2025 + F&O FY26 · 145 Groww Play reviews · competitor map
Brief: "create a Groww App suited for GenZ users" — so this is a Groww app, not a feature and not a startup.

The call

We are building the Groww app a 22-year-old gets after KYC clears. Same company, same brand, same products — a different default surface, chosen for someone with their first paycheck instead of someone with a trading account.

The current prototype is a single flow that ends at "done". That was right for testing one hypothesis and it tested well with the in-ICP user. It is not a Groww app. The three things it is missing are the three things the research keeps pointing at: a home you come back to, a reason to make the second investment, and a way to check the app instead of trusting it.

The metric, restated

Not signups. Not first investment. Second investment within 30 days. Everything below is scoped to that, which is also what lets us put F&O, lending, IPOs and tax filing out of scope with a straight face.

In scope

Post-KYC first investment · the home you return to · cost shown before every trade · the red day · the second investment · plain-language explainers on anything tappable · a visible statement of what this app deliberately does not have.

Out of scope — and each has a reason, not just a shrug

Act 1 · 1 screen

The wall we're replacing

Opens the prototype. Makes the argument in five seconds, before any of our design has to.

00Groww today, post-loginNew

A faithful, static recreation of what a first-timer actually lands on: the tab row (Stocks · F&O · Mutual Funds · Commodities), a Fast Exit F&O banner, a personal-loan card, a "trending stocks" strip. One button: "I have ₹5,000. What do I do?" — which nothing on the screen answers.

Tapping anywhere goes to Screen 01.

Evidence. Groww's own Play listing leads with "Exit your F&O positions instantly", "Pledge your stock holdings", "Personal Loans — APR: 13–48%". Reviews: "the F&O page remains the initial landing page, regardless of the fact that I don't use the F&O feature"; "it will create F&O, Bonds accounts without your concern". Meanwhile the thing people praise is the beginner experience. The asset is being spent to chase traders — that tension is the whole case study.

Act 2 · 6 screens

The first investment

The existing flow, with the four defects the interviews found, plus the one question the survey now demands.

01What's this money for?Keep

Unchanged. Five goals in months, not decades — nothing specific · a trip · a phone/laptop · a buffer · the long run.

Evidence. 20% of non-investors reject investing because "it's for long term". Goal-wise tracking is the top feature request in the Play reviews. Both participants passed this screen without friction.
02When might you need it back?Keep

Unchanged, and it is the load-bearing screen. Under a year · 1–3 years · 3+ years · no idea.

Evidence. This is why the red day worked on Prakhar — "mujhe isko dekh ke kuch tension nahi ho rahi hai… kyunki main already sure tha ki main ek dedh saal rukunga." Calm because the horizon was set first.
03How much — now recommended, not askedRework

Today this screen asks a blank question and offers ₹100 / ₹500 / ₹1,000 / ₹5,000. The updated survey says that's backwards.

We'd start you at ₹500. Small enough that a bad month doesn't matter. Big enough that the charges don't eat it. You can change this any time, and stop any month. [ ₹500 — start here ] ← primary ₹100 ₹1,000 ₹5,000 ← secondary, still one tap

Same four amounts. One of them is now a recommendation with a reason attached.

Evidence. At n=24, "how much should I be investing" became the single most-cited thing nobody ever explained — 12 of 24, ahead of MF-vs-index-vs-ETF (11), charges (11) and tax (11). The app currently asks the exact question the sample says it wants answered. Also kills Prakhar's SIP misconception in one line — he refused a SIP only because "phir mereko monthly paanch hazaar dena padega na".
04How much of it can wobble?New

One screen, three taps. Not a risk questionnaire — a split.

Of your ₹5,000, how much are you happy to see move around? ○ None of it → all steady ● A small slice → ₹4,500 steady + ₹500 you can swing with ○ I'm here for it → ₹3,500 steady + ₹1,500 swing The steady part is the recommendation. The slice is yours to pick — and we'll show you what it costs either way.

This is the one genuinely new mechanic, and it does three jobs at once.

Evidence. (1) Pulin raised risk appetite four separate times unprompted — "pehle to mera risk appetite poochna chahiye kisi ko, agar koi mujhe help kar raha hai to" — and proposed this exact mechanic: "apne risk ko partition karke, ki thoda sa risk mein lagao, baaki thoda safe rakho". (2) It answers his "not enough questions… it's too shallow" without breaking the three-question promise — it makes it four. (3) It is the dossier's "safe home for the thrill": CFA finds 61% of Gen Z investors gamble vs 29% of non-investors, and right now the only exciting thing in Groww is F&O, which takes 89% of under-30s who touch it. A capped, named, visible slice is somewhere for that to go that isn't options.
05Our pick, and how it's actually doneRework

Keeps the named fund, the verified expense ratio and the 3/5/10-year past returns added yesterday. Two changes:

  • Move the argument next to the table, not below it. Pulin read the numbers, saw large-cap funds beating our index fund, and closed the screen — he never reached the "the 0.25% is certain, their returns are not" line. Counter-evidence and the answer to it have to arrive together.
  • Show the split from Screen 04 — steady part and swing part, priced separately.
Evidence. "It showed me numbers better but I didn't like the reasoning." — Pulin, 23:58. And on the honest side: UTI Nifty 50 is at +5.6% / +6.7% / +11.3% against a large-cap category average of +15.1% / +15.0% / +13.2%. We show that. The argument has to be strong enough to survive showing it.
06What this costsRework

Three fixes, all from the interviews:

  • Kill the comparison bug. Today the "active fund" tab silently switches from ₹5,000 once to ₹5,000/month × 120 months. Pulin: "comparison hi fuddu hai." Run it on the amount and frequency the user actually picked.
  • No initialisms. "DP charge" becomes "Depository fee when you sell — a flat ₹15.93, the same on ₹500 as on ₹1 lakh." Pulin read "DP" and guessed "Direct plan" — on the one line the whole argument rests on.
  • Add the decision, not just the number. He got ₹40 right and concluded "this forty is unavoidable, right?" So the screen has to close the loop: "Unavoidable on a stock. On the fund we picked it's ₹6.28, because there's no depository fee. That's the whole reason we didn't start you in a stock."
Evidence. 17 of 24 wrong on charges, 15 badly under, and nobody picked "no idea" — everyone was confident and wrong. Prakhar read the ₹43 as money he'd receive. Pulin learned the number and kept the behaviour. This screen has failed differently on every person who has seen it.

Act 3 · 4 screens — the biggest addition

The app you come back to

Everything above already existed. This is what makes it a Groww app instead of a flow, and it's where the actual metric lives.

07Done — and what happens nextRework

Keep the seconds-to-invested timer (it's the demo's best moment and an eval measure). Add one thing: the date of the second investment, set now, while intent is high.

Done. You're invested. 58s from opening the app Next: we'll ask you again on 1 Oct. Same ₹500, one tap, no decisions. [ Change ] [ Not now ]
Evidence. Prakhar's account died in exactly this gap — "demat account activate hone mein time laga tha… us time ke wajah se main distract ho gaya." Not fear, not cost. A delay long enough to lose the thread. The second investment is the metric; ask for it while they're still here.
08Home — what you own, in wordsNew

The screen that doesn't exist today, and the reason this is an app. Deliberately not a returns dashboard.

Your money ₹500 in UTI Nifty 50 Index Fund ₹502.10 ▲ ₹2.10 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── You own a sliver of 50 companies. Bought 17 Sep. You said you need it after 2029. Charges so far: ₹0.31 ← always visible, never a surprise Next: ₹500 on 1 Oct [ Do it now ] ───────────────────────────────────────────────────── Today's 10-minute read → "What a Nifty 50 actually is"
Evidence. SEBI names poor performance as 87% of dormancy — a home that leads with a red number manufactures the churn. Leading with what you own and why you bought it puts the horizon back on screen every time they open the app. Prakhar never reopened Groww after submitting KYC; this is the screen that would have given him a reason to.
09The red dayKeep

Essentially unchanged — it is the best-performing screen in both interviews. Prakhar read the 2008 and March 2020 recovery figures out loud and chose "do nothing" on his own. Pulin: "of course do nothing", then volunteered "waiting is the best thing ever".

One addition: reachable from Home, not only from a "skip ahead" button, so it reads as part of the app rather than a demo device.

Evidence. 9 of 24 say nobody ever explained what to do when it's showing a loss. Both participants handled it correctly. Don't touch what works.
10The second investmentNew

Two weeks on. One tap, no decisions re-asked — this is where "the app remembers you" becomes visible.

Welcome back. Still 3+ years, still ₹500 a month? [ Yes, do it ] [ Change ] One thing changed since you bought: your fund's fee dropped from 0.25% to 0.23%. That's it. Nothing else needs your attention.
Evidence. This is the demoable half of your "recursive AI" idea — it does not need a learning model, it needs a second session that visibly doesn't re-ask. Directly answers Pulin's "he's leading himself and not letting me lead" and is literally the success metric.

Act 4 · 2 screens

Teach me to check you

The strongest cross-interview finding, and no competitor ships against it.

Prakhar: "I don't know which is the source of truth… agar tera investing app hai to main sirf tere AI pe reliable nahi hona chahiye. Mere ko baaki jagah se bhi information milni chahiye."

Pulin: "Jin factors pe market khelta hai, wo agar mujhe koi bataaye taaki main khud dekh paun — kyunki main apne aap se zyada kisko trust karunga? Kisi ko bhi nahi."

Two of two, unprompted, in the closing minutes of both interviews
11Tap anything to understand itNew

Every jargon term anywhere in the app is tappable. Opens a sheet, not a page. Three parts: plain words · the number on your screen right now · where to check it yourself.

Depository fee A flat ₹15.93 charged when you sell shares. It goes to CDSL, not to Groww, and it's the same whether you sell ₹500 or ₹1 lakh. On your ₹500: that's 3.2% — which is why we didn't start you in a stock. Check it yourself → CDSL published tariff · SEBI charges page
Evidence. "Need a tutor, that helps me with this stuff from scratch" — survey free text. 11 of 24 say nobody explained MF vs index vs ETF vs stock, 11 say charges, 11 say tax, 10 say when to sell. Pulin: "seedha jargon mere munh pe na fenke… jisko index fund hi nahi pata, usko kya samajh mein aayega?" The third line is what makes it answer the source-of-truth need rather than adding another voice to trust.
12The ten-minute readNew

One card on Home, daily. Not a course, not a tab. Sized to a commute, and always about something you own.

Evidence. Pulin handed us the format: "har din notification aa raha hai ki ye aaj padh lo, ten minute read hai… and let's say within one month I am actually able to do things on my own. I would be very grateful." He had already tried and quit Zerodha Varsity — "hafte mahine ka investment hai". Education tabs go unopened; Instagram gets 14 of 24. The dossier's rule: learning after the money, attached to what they own.

Act 5 · 1 screen

What this app doesn't have

13The absences, statedNew

Reachable from Home. Currently this argument only exists as a sentence inside an expander — in a real app shell, the absence is the design statement.

Things we left out, on purpose F&O and intraday 43% of F&O traders are under 30. 89% of them lost money last year. Personal loans Not next to your first investment. Trending stocks A list of what went up recently is not advice. Notifications Only when something needs you. Never to sell you.
Evidence. Users leave Groww for Zerodha citing "no pushing of any stuff" and "I always feel like I am in control". 29 of 145 reviews complain about ads and upsell. And this is a live disagreement with named companies — Millions ships F&O at ₹20/order; Trackk's flagship is "F&O in the time it takes to make chai." Nobody has taken the other side.

Build order, with a cut line

If everything ships, 14 screens. If it doesn't, cut from the bottom.

What I'd cut first, honestly

12 (one static card proves the idea), then 11 down to just the three terms that actually broke — depository fee, expense ratio, exit load. Don't cut 08 or 10; without them this is still a flow, not an app, and the brief asked for an app.

Five things I want you to push back on

  1. Screen 04, the swing slice. It's the only new mechanic and it cuts against "one decision, pre-made". It's justified by Pulin — who is out of ICP. Prakhar never asked for it. Real risk it's a screen for the wrong user.
  2. Screen 00. Recreating today's Groww to criticise it is a strong opening or a cheap shot, depending on execution. Your call.
  3. How far to take Groww's visual identity. Using their green and their type on a fake app that criticises them is a real question. My instinct: Groww's layout patterns and structure, our own wordmark, a visible "concept" label. You said you want it to look like Groww — tell me how close.
  4. 13 screens may be too many. Every screen is defensible on its own; together they may rebuild the catalogue we're deleting. If you want it tighter, 00/12/13 go first.
  5. We have no in-ICP user left to test on. Prakhar has seen the old build; Pulin is activated. Interview 3 should be someone with a Groww account who has never invested — 7 of 24 in the survey fit, and 16 left contact details.
